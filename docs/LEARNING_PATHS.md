# Learning paths

[Start](../README.md) · [Assessment](ASSESSMENT.md) · [Industrial AI map](INDUSTRIAL_AI.md) · [Catalogue](CATALOG.md)

Pick **one destination**. Hours below are proposed allocations for selected sections and our exercises, not the complete provider courses. Prerequisites are additional. An exit check, rather than elapsed time, determines readiness. Resource IDs link to access information; tools and hardware are examples, not purchasing recommendations.

For AI at work, use an assistant already permitted in your environment. For developing AI, start with a small local example and a simple baseline. Every route has a reading or simulation option; distinguish it from a measured deployment.

## P0 AI orientation

**For:** anyone choosing where AI belongs in their work. **Prerequisites:** none. **Allocation:** 4 h.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C01](CATALOG.md#c01): selected AI concepts and limitations | 2 |
| 2 | Read the [brain analogy](INDUSTRIAL_AI.md) and the workflow choices in [R01](CATALOG.md#r01) | 1 |
| 3 | Write a use-case brief: input, output, baseline, owner, acceptance check | 1 |

**Exit:** explain the distinction between using and building AI; place one example in each brain role; identify a task better served by a normal script or lookup. Choose W1–W4 for daily work or D1–D4 for development. Optional business context: [C21–C24](CATALOG.md#c21), outside the four hours.

## P1 Programming foundations

**For:** learners who need to read, write, and test small programs. **Prerequisites:** none. **Allocation:** 24 h; a complete beginner may need much longer.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C02](CATALOG.md#c02): functions, exceptions, files, tests; alternatively start with [C03](CATALOG.md#c03) | 16 |
| 2 | [C04](CATALOG.md#c04): shell, Git, diffs, debugging | 4 |
| 3 | [B1 sensor-data contract](PRACTICE.md#b1-sensor-data-contract), implemented manually first | 4 |

**Exit:** independently explain an input error, reproduce a test failure, fix it, and review a small diff. If C03 is chosen, add test-writing practice before leaving. Experienced programmers can demonstrate this directly and skip the introductory study.

## W1 Knowledge work and copilots

**For:** project teams, technical specialists, managers, and office users. **Prerequisites:** ability to judge the subject matter; no coding. **Allocation:** 6 h.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C33](CATALOG.md#c33): prompting and one relevant office application | 2 |
| 2 | [R12](CATALOG.md#r12): write a request with goal, source, context, expected output | 1 |
| 3 | [B6 source-grounded knowledge work](PRACTICE.md#b6-source-grounded-knowledge-work) | 2 |
| 4 | Peer-check facts and compare time including rework | 1 |

**Exit:** produce a useful brief with traceable claims and explicit unknowns; check arithmetic independently. No licence: use the fictional documents and critique a draft on paper. Do not treat a tool's ability to retrieve a document as permission to share it.

## W2 CAD and mechanical design

**For:** mechanical engineers and CAD users applying AI to design work. **Prerequisites:** loads, constraints, materials, and basic CAD. **Allocation:** 8 h; mechanical fundamentals are additional.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C31](CATALOG.md#c31): study setup and constraints | 3 |
| 2 | [B7 fictional bracket](PRACTICE.md#b7-cad-design-review): define requirements and baseline geometry | 2 |
| 3 | Compare alternatives against the same assumptions; inspect manufacturability | 2 |
| 4 | Explain why a candidate should be accepted, rejected, or investigated | 1 |

**Exit:** a constraint sheet, alternatives table, and verification plan. Distinguish generative optimization from an LLM producing a CAD script. Without CAD/cloud-solve access, use the supplied hypothetical outcomes and label the exercise a design review, not a validated part. A qualified reviewer is needed if the learner lacks domain foundations.

## W3 Electronics and PCB design

**For:** electronics/ECAD users applying AI to existing engineering tasks. **Prerequisites:** circuit fundamentals and datasheet reading. **Allocation:** 8 h; introductory electronics is additional.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Selected [C32](CATALOG.md#c32) schematics/library material; experienced users review briefly | 2 |
| 2 | [R10](CATALOG.md#r10): AI-assisted component/datasheet workflow | 1 |
| 3 | [B8 component review](PRACTICE.md#b8-electronics-component-review), with units and evidence | 3 |
| 4 | Peer review; explain what ERC/DRC and simulation can and cannot establish | 2 |

**Exit:** a checked comparison and unresolved-questions list, not an unreviewed schematic generated by AI. No tool account: use the fictional component sheets. AI does not replace the domain foundation; C32 is explicitly a PCB course, not an AI course.

## W4 Agentic coding

**For:** developers using coding assistants in daily work. **Prerequisites:** P1 exit check or equivalent; basic Git. **Allocation:** 16 h.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C12](CATALOG.md#c12): codebase context, scoped features, debugging/testing | 3 |
| 2 | [R03](CATALOG.md#r03), [R04](CATALOG.md#r04): verifiable tasks and repeatable checks | 2 |
| 3 | [B1](PRACTICE.md#b1-sensor-data-contract): manual baseline, agent-assisted revision, held-back cases | 7 |
| 4 | Independent diff review, log analysis, and a five-minute handover | 4 |

**Exit:** requirements-linked tests, a reviewable diff, a record of failed attempts, and an honest time/cost comparison. Set an attempt cap and keep acceptance tests outside the agent's edit scope. Stop a looping agent when it cannot make progress. No assistant: pair one learner as implementer and one as reviewer; this teaches the workflow but does not measure model performance.

Already comfortable? Use [C13](CATALOG.md#c13) for a deeper software-engineering discussion. Building the agent itself is the [D3](#d3-agentic-ai-and-loops) route, not a prerequisite for using one.

## D1 Embedded AI

**Brain role:** reflex. **For:** developers embedding inference in a constrained device. **Prerequisites:** Python, introductory ML, signal sampling, and C/C++ buffers/firmware tools for real deployment. Refresh with P1 and [C06](CATALOG.md#c06) as needed. **Allocation:** 24 h after prerequisites.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C28](CATALOG.md#c28): ML, preprocessing, deployment/anomaly sections | 8 |
| 2 | [R11](CATALOG.md#r11): model conversion and runtime/resource concepts | 4 |
| 3 | [B4 embedded classifier](PRACTICE.md#b4-embedded-reflex): baseline, conversion, target or emulator | 8 |
| 4 | Review accuracy change, RAM/flash, latency, and failure cases | 4 |

**Exit:** reproducible signal processing and a deployment report distinguishing measured values from estimates. A simulation passes the conceptual milestone only. Target readiness requires measurements on the actual device, including runtime memory and representative timing; classifier speed does not establish hard real-time or safety suitability.

## D2 Edge AI

**Brain role:** local perception and coordination. **For:** developers deploying inference near a machine/cell. **Prerequisites:** Python, basic ML, Linux, and packaging/container basics for deployment. **Allocation:** 24 h after prerequisites.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C29](CATALOG.md#c29): local inference and one vision tutorial appropriate to available hardware | 6 |
| 2 | [C18](CATALOG.md#c18): selected packaging, deployment, monitoring topics | 6 |
| 3 | [B5 local inspection pipeline](PRACTICE.md#b5-edge-perception-pipeline): replay, buffer, infer, log | 8 |
| 4 | Exercise network loss, restart, stale inputs, and model rollback | 4 |

**Exit:** an end-to-end latency report and repeatable failure/recovery demonstration. No Jetson required for the first local replay exercise; laptop results cannot substantiate target-device performance. Add a local agent only when adaptive decisions add value and evaluate it separately.

## D3 Agentic AI and loops

**Brain role:** planning and tool use. **For:** developers building agent applications. **Prerequisites:** Python, APIs, testing; P1 exit check or equivalent. **Allocation:** 24 h.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C11](CATALOG.md#c11): agent fundamentals and one implementation; [C10](CATALOG.md#c10) is an optional video alternative | 6 |
| 2 | [C14](CATALOG.md#c14): tool interface concepts and a local example | 4 |
| 3 | [C16](CATALOG.md#c16), [R05](CATALOG.md#r05): dataset, traces, error analysis | 4 |
| 4 | [B2 bounded maintenance assistant](PRACTICE.md#b2-bounded-maintenance-assistant): compare fixed workflow to agent | 8 |
| 5 | Review stop conditions, tool failures, and held-out results | 2 |

**Exit:** a source-grounded answer, a trace of tool use, budget enforcement, and evidence on both answerable and unanswerable cases. Separate changes suggested by an agent from the decision to execute them. Live model performance must be measured; simulated traces demonstrate control flow only.

Read [R02](CATALOG.md#r02) during the exercise. Distinguish a model's tool-use loop, a code-repair loop, a dataset/evaluation improvement cycle, and a scheduled trigger. Scheduling does not improve a bad stopping rule. Start with a single agent; consider [C15](CATALOG.md#c15) or multiple agents only when traces expose a specific need. No path depends on the retiring OpenAI Evals platform.

## D4 Industrial data and ML

**For:** analysts and developers evaluating engineering data. **Prerequisites:** Python and basic statistics. **Allocation:** 24 h.

| Order | Study or practice | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | [C06](CATALOG.md#c06): regression/classification and overfitting; MATLAB users can begin with [C30](CATALOG.md#c30) | 4 |
| 2 | [C07](CATALOG.md#c07): pipelines, validation, model selection | 8 |
| 3 | [B3 condition-monitoring study](PRACTICE.md#b3-industrial-data-baseline) | 8 |
| 4 | Error analysis and a short presentation | 4 |

**Exit:** compare a simple baseline and model on a justified split, explain false alarms/missed faults, and identify where the experiment does not generalize. C30 substitutes introductory material only; C07 uses Python. Optional depth: [C08](CATALOG.md#c08) for vision, [R07](CATALOG.md#r07) for forecasting, [C26](CATALOG.md#c26) for uncertainty, [C27](CATALOG.md#c27) for causality. Take D1/D2 when deployment becomes the goal.

## T1 Teaching and facilitation

**For:** team learning organizers and lecturers. **Prerequisites:** ability to explain the selected example; complete its relevant path or pair with a domain expert before teaching a technical lab. **Allocation:** 6 h preparation, excluding your own prerequisite learning.

| Order | Preparation | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Review learners' voluntary [assessment](ASSESSMENT.md) needs and choose a track | 1 |
| 2 | Rehearse a [practice brief](PRACTICE.md), including a failure and the paper fallback | 2 |
| 3 | Adapt the [90-minute session or lecture sequence](TEACHING.md) and rubric | 2 |
| 4 | Check links/access and ask a colleague to review the material | 1 |

**Exit:** a worked example, a learner exercise, expected evidence, and a debrief. Grade understanding and verification, not subscription access or prompt fluency. Respect third-party course licences; link to materials and use original local exercises for the internal workshop.
