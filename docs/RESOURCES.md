## How this repository complements existing lists

Reviewed **2026-10-24**, based on the repositories' current READMEs. The comparison describes emphasis, not a feature audit of every linked page. Their breadth is useful; we link to it instead of copying their inventories.

| Collection | Observed emphasis | Use it when | What this guide adds for our audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| [prakhar1989/awesome-courses](https://github.com/prakhar1989/awesome-courses) | University computer-science courses, with lectures, notes, assignments, and exams across theory and systems | You need academic depth beyond a short practical route | A starting assessment, bounded study blocks, and industrial practice outcomes |
| [mahseema/awesome-ai-tools](https://github.com/mahseema/awesome-ai-tools) | AI products grouped by activity, including code, text, images, and learning resources | You want to discover candidate tools | Course/practice sequences and evidence of learning; product discovery is not an endorsement or permission to use company data |
| [owainlewis/awesome-artificial-intelligence](https://github.com/owainlewis/awesome-artificial-intelligence) | Opinionated technical resources for generative AI, production systems, agents, evaluation, and coding workflows | You want broader engineering references and current ecosystem coverage | A specific teaching frame: Industrial AI, using versus building, the brain analogy, learner routing, and reusable workshops |
| [hades217/awesome-ai](https://github.com/hades217/awesome-ai) | Broad AI ecosystem resources including tools, courses, and communities | You want to continue exploring the original repository's AI reference | A smaller destination-oriented selection rather than another ecosystem directory |

The owainlewis collection already discusses practical value, quality, evaluation, and curation. Calling this repository merely “practical” or “curated” would not distinguish it. The useful proposition is: **help a colleague find the right first block, apply it to an industrial example, and show what they learned.** That proposition remains a hypothesis until learners use it.

## What to optimize

| Design choice | Why it matters | What a pilot can tell us |
|---|---|---|
| AI at work versus developing AI | A CAD user should not be required to train an LLM before getting value | Did the assessment recommend a route matching the person's actual task? |
| Embedded / edge / agentic brain analogy | Gives mixed teams a common discussion model without hiding deployment differences | Can learners explain a combined example and the metaphor's limits? |
| Small resource set per path | Limits time spent choosing among similar courses | Time from opening the guide to choosing a first study block |
| Domain-specific evidence | Completion means more than watching videos | Can a peer reproduce or check the learner's output? |
| Open/simulation alternatives | Supports colleagues and classrooms with different access | How often do accounts, cost, or hardware block the first exercise? |
| Honest access/review labels | Prevents misleading “free” claims | Number of reported access mismatches and time to correct them |

No benchmark results or adoption figures are claimed. Begin with a small voluntary team pilot, collect feedback after the first session and two weeks of study, then adjust routing and workload. Keep individual scores private. Prefer a few well-supported paths to a large unreviewed tool inventory.

## Scope discipline

Keep mathematical, software, and business foundations discoverable through the catalogue and the [complete original-link audit](RESEARCH.md#original-link-audit). Promote an elective into a default path only when it solves a demonstrated learner need. Keep articles, docs, product hubs, historical lectures, and structured courses visibly distinct. Cite and link other curators; do not import their marketing descriptions, rankings, or lists wholesale.
